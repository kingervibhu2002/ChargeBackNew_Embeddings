# Amex M10 — Vehicle Rental Dispute

## What This Dispute Code Means

American Express dispute code M10 covers disputes arising from vehicle rental transactions. A cardholder may dispute a rental charge because the final invoice amount exceeds what they authorized, additional charges appeared after the rental (damage fees, fuel fees, late return fees), they believe damage charges are fraudulent, or they dispute supplemental fees they did not agree to.

Vehicle rental disputes are among the most document-intensive in the chargeback ecosystem. Rental companies handle physical assets that are inherently prone to incremental damage, and the gap between what the renter believes they returned and what the rental company subsequently charges can be significant. Amex cardholders who drive premium rental vehicles tend to be high-value customers who escalate disputes quickly — making robust documentation at every stage of the rental critical.

---

## Types of Charges That Generate M10 Disputes

**Damage charges**: The cardholder believes damage they are being billed for was pre-existing or was caused by another party. This is the most common and most contested M10 category.

**Fuel charges**: The vehicle was returned with less fuel than at pickup, and the cardholder disputes the fuel fee — either the amount or whether the deficiency occurred.

**Late return fees**: The cardholder returned the vehicle after the agreed time and disputes the additional day or late hour fee.

**Additional driver fees**: Fees for drivers added to the rental agreement are disputed as not agreed to.

**Insurance/protection charges**: Collision damage waiver (CDW), supplemental liability protection, or other insurance products were added without the cardholder's consent.

**Drop-off fees**: One-way rental drop-off charges the cardholder claims were not disclosed.

**Mileage overage fees**: The cardholder disputes mileage charges on what they believed was an unlimited mileage rental.

## The Foundation: The Signed Rental Agreement

Every component of an M10 defense starts with the signed rental agreement. This document — whether physical or electronic — establishes what the cardholder agreed to pay and under what conditions.

**A complete rental agreement should document:**
- Renter name and Amex card number (last four digits)
- Rental period (pickup date/time, agreed return date/time)
- Vehicle make, model, year, and license plate
- Agreed rental rate (daily/weekly, applicable discounts)
- Additional driver authorizations and fees
- Insurance/protection selections and waiver status
- Fuel policy (full-to-full, prepaid, or first-tank-free)
- Mileage terms (unlimited or cap with per-mile rate)
- One-way/drop-off fee terms
- Cardholder signature (or digital acknowledgment)

If your rental agreement does not include all these elements, or if the cardholder disputes signing it, your M10 defense becomes significantly more difficult.

For electronic rental agreements, preserve the digital signature record including the IP address, timestamp, and authentication method.

## Pre-Existing Damage Documentation: The Most Critical Practice

The most common and most costly M10 disputes involve damage fees. The cardholder believes the damage was there before they picked up the vehicle; the rental company claims the damage occurred during the rental. Without contemporaneous documentation of vehicle condition at pickup, these disputes are very difficult to win.

### Pre-Rental Inspection Documentation

Every rental should be accompanied by a **vehicle condition report** completed at the time of pickup:

- A schematic diagram of the vehicle with pre-existing damage marked (scratches, dents, chips)
- Photos of every exterior panel, including close-ups of any existing damage
- Photos of the interior (seat condition, dashboard, carpets)
- Fuel level at pickup (dashboard photo or gauge reading on inspection form)
- Odometer reading at pickup
- Renter's signature acknowledging the condition report at pickup

If the renter signs a clean condition report, they are acknowledging there is no pre-existing damage (beyond what is marked). If they refuse to sign, note this on the form and have a staff witness sign.

### Post-Rental Inspection Documentation

At return:
- Complete the same vehicle condition report format
- Photos of every exterior panel after return
- Photos of any new damage compared to the pickup photos
- Odometer reading at return
- Fuel level at return (dashboard photo)
- Staff inspector name and signature on the return condition report
- Date and time of return inspection (showing vehicle was checked promptly after return)

For damage disputes, the photographic comparison between pre-rental and post-rental condition is your primary evidence. If a photo taken at 9:00am shows no damage to the front bumper, and a photo taken at 3:00pm (after the renter returned the vehicle at 2:00pm) shows a dent, the photographic record is highly compelling.

## Fuel Policy Evidence

Fuel disputes are prevented by clear documentation of:

- **Fuel level at pickup**: Dashboard gauge photo or marked on inspection form
- **Fuel policy disclosed and signed**: The agreement clearly states the policy (full-to-full, or prepaid at $X.XX/gallon)
- **Fuel level at return**: Dashboard gauge photo or reading by staff
- **Fuel charge calculation**: If a fuel surcharge was applied, show the calculation (gallons × rate per gallon)

For prepaid fuel packages, confirm the cardholder accepted the package and the option to return full-tank was clearly available.

---

## Additional Charges: Disclosure Is Everything

For any charge beyond the base rental rate — insurance products, additional driver fees, GPS rental, child seat rental, drop-off fees, mileage overages — the disclosure and acceptance in the signed rental agreement is your evidence.

If a charge appears on the final invoice but was not in the signed rental agreement, you will almost certainly lose that portion of an M10 dispute. Apply additional charges only when they were explicitly agreed to in the rental agreement or in a subsequent signed amendment.

---

## Drop-Off Confirmation

Late return and one-way drop-off disputes require proof that the vehicle was returned at the date, time, and location agreed. Evidence:

- **Return date/time record** from your fleet management or vehicle return system
- **Return location confirmation** (rental location address where vehicle was received)
- **Staff confirmation record** (name of employee who accepted the return)
- **One-way drop-off agreement** showing the cardholder agreed to the one-way fee at booking

For disputes about after-hours returns (drop box returns), provide keybox return records or timestamp from your vehicle access system showing when the key was returned.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The renter returned the car with damage and disputes our damage charge. How do I win?**
A: Pre-rental and post-rental inspection photos are your primary evidence. Show a before photo (at pickup) with no damage in that area, and an after photo (at return) with the damage. Include the signed condition report at pickup where the renter acknowledged the vehicle's pre-existing condition. If the renter signed a clean condition report and damage appears in your post-rental photos, your case is strong.

**Q: The cardholder claims they pre-purchased CDW through their credit card and should not be charged our collision waiver. Is this a valid M10?**
A: This is a common dispute. Amex Platinum and some other Amex cards include car rental coverage. If the cardholder did not waive their card's coverage and declined your CDW on the rental agreement, they have a valid basis for the dispute if you charged your CDW anyway. However, if the cardholder accepted your CDW in the signed rental agreement, that acceptance is generally binding regardless of their card coverage. Review the rental agreement carefully.

**Q: We charged an additional day because the vehicle was returned two hours late. The cardholder disputes this. How do I defend it?**
A: Submit your vehicle return records showing the timestamp of the vehicle's return (GPS fleet tracking, key return log, or staff check-in record), the agreed return time from the rental agreement, and your late return fee policy as disclosed in the agreement. An objective timestamp showing the return occurred after the agreed time, combined with a disclosed fee schedule, is a strong defense.

**Q: The renter photographed pre-existing damage at pickup that we failed to document. Can they use their own photos against us?**
A: Yes. If the renter's timestamped photos show damage at pickup that you then charge them for, those photos are valid dispute evidence. This is why your pre-rental inspection must be thorough and documented by your staff — relying on the renter's failure to document pre-existing damage leaves you vulnerable. A comprehensive pre-rental photo record protects both parties.

**Q: We charge a premium insurance product at pickup that the renter accepted verbally but the rental agreement only references it as "optional protection selected." Is that sufficient?**
A: Marginally. A line item on the signed rental agreement identifying the product and its daily rate is better than a verbal acknowledgment. If the rental agreement shows the insurance product line and the cardholder's signature, you have written evidence of acceptance. For high-value insurance products, ensure the acceptance is a checkbox or explicit selection on the agreement, not simply an unsigned line item.
