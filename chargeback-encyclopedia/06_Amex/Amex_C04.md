# Amex C04 — Goods or Services Cancelled

## What This Dispute Code Means

Amex dispute code C04 is filed when a cardholder states they cancelled an order or reservation and the merchant either continued to charge them or failed to issue a refund following the cancellation. This dispute covers a broad range of cancellation scenarios: cancelled product orders, cancelled service appointments, cancelled hotel or travel reservations, and early terminations of subscription services.

The dispute is fundamentally about what your cancellation policy says, whether it was clearly disclosed, and whether the cancellation was made within the terms of that policy. Merchants who maintain clear, prominently disclosed cancellation policies and who respond to cancellations promptly and correctly are well-positioned to defend C04 disputes — or to prevent them entirely.

---

## Types of Cancellation Scenarios

### Pre-Shipment Order Cancellation

The cardholder placed an order, requested cancellation before the item shipped, and the merchant either charged the card or failed to refund it. This is one of the most straightforward scenarios — if the item had not yet been prepared or shipped, merchants generally have limited grounds to retain the payment.

**Merchant wins when:** The order was already in production (custom items), packaged, or at a point where cancellation incurs genuine costs that were disclosed in the cancellation policy.

**Merchant loses when:** The order was easily cancellable, no cancellation fee was disclosed, and the merchant simply declined to cancel.

### Service Appointment Cancellation

The cardholder booked a service (hair appointment, landscaping, cleaning, consulting session) and cancelled before the appointment date. Cancellation fee policies vary widely — some services charge nothing for cancellations made 48+ hours in advance, others charge 50% or full price for last-minute cancellations.

**Merchant wins when:** The cancellation was made inside a clearly disclosed cancellation window (e.g., "cancellations within 24 hours are charged 100%"), the policy was visible at booking, and the cardholder agreed to it.

**Merchant loses when:** The policy was buried in fine print, not disclosed at the time of booking, or was applied to a cancellation that occurred outside the fee window.

### Subscription Cancellation

The cardholder cancelled a recurring subscription but was charged for another billing period. This frequently occurs at the boundary of billing cycles — the cardholder cancels on Day 29 of a 30-day cycle, the charge for the next month has already processed, and they dispute it.

**Merchant wins when:** Your cancellation terms clearly state that cancellations take effect at the end of the current billing period, the cardholder agreed to these terms, and the cancellation was processed correctly per those terms.

**Merchant loses when:** The cardholder cancelled and you continued to charge beyond the agreed billing cycle end, or the cancellation portal failed to process their request.

### Event or Travel Cancellation

The cardholder purchased event tickets or travel services and the event was cancelled, or the merchant cancelled the service and failed to issue a refund.

**Merchant wins when:** A clearly disclosed non-refundable policy was accepted at purchase, the event was not cancelled (only the cardholder chose not to attend), and the no-show or no-cancel terms applied.

**Merchant loses when:** The merchant cancelled the event or service and failed to refund the cardholder, or when the refund policy was not clearly communicated at purchase.

## Evidence That Wins C04 Disputes

### Cancellation Policy Documentation

The most important evidence is proof that your cancellation policy was:
1. Clearly displayed at the point of purchase or booking
2. Accepted by the cardholder (via checkbox, signature, or affirmative click)
3. Applicable to the specific cancellation scenario

Submit screenshots of your booking flow showing where the policy appeared, and if possible, a timestamped record of the cardholder's acceptance (checked "I agree to the cancellation policy" box).

### No Cancellation Request Received

If the cardholder claims they cancelled but you have no record of a cancellation request:
- Show your cancellation portal logs (no cancellation submitted through your system)
- Show email records (no cancellation email received)
- Show call logs (no call from the cardholder's phone number)

The absence of a cancellation record, combined with a clear statement of how cancellations must be submitted, can rebut the cardholder's claim.

### Confirmation of Policy Display

For online services, show that the cancellation policy was:
- Displayed on the checkout page
- Included in the booking confirmation email
- Part of the terms the cardholder accepted

For in-person bookings, show signed agreement, appointment card, or receipt that includes the cancellation terms.

### Refund Processing (If Within Policy)

If the cancellation was valid under your policy and a refund was owed, show that the refund was processed promptly. Combine this with the refund transaction ID. A C04 where the merchant issued the correct refund quickly is often closed in the merchant's favor.

## No-Show Policies and C04

No-show situations — where the cardholder made a reservation and failed to appear without cancelling — are a common source of C04 disputes. Hotels, restaurants, and service businesses routinely charge no-show fees.

Amex will support a no-show fee when:
- The no-show fee was disclosed at booking (displayed on the reservation form and in the confirmation email)
- The cardholder accepted the policy at time of booking
- The merchant has a record that no cancellation was received before the deadline
- The merchant charged only the disclosed no-show fee, not additional amounts

## When to Voluntarily Issue Refunds vs. Fight

**Fight the C04 when:**
- You have clear documentation that the policy was disclosed, accepted, and correctly applied
- The cardholder cancelled outside the free cancellation window
- No cancellation request was ever submitted through your system

**Voluntarily refund when:**
- You cannot demonstrate the policy was clearly disclosed at the time of booking
- The cancellation was made within your stated free-cancellation window
- Your system failed to process the cardholder's cancellation request
- The service was cancelled by you (not the cardholder) and no refund was issued

Fighting C04 with weak or no policy disclosure documentation is almost always a loss — and adds the dispute to your chargeback ratio. If you cannot document the policy's clarity, refunding is the better outcome.

## Cancellation Window Rules and Timing

Amex's rules specify that cardholders can file C04 if they cancelled within your stated cancellation policy but were still charged. Your policy terms must be legally and contractually clear on:

- **When the cancellation window opens and closes** (e.g., "cancellations accepted until 48 hours before service")
- **How cancellations must be submitted** (online portal, email, phone — and the required contact information)
- **What the fee structure is** for cancellations at different time intervals
- **What constitutes a valid cancellation confirmation** (reference number, confirmation email)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The customer claims they cancelled by email but I never received it. Who wins?**
A: This depends on your cancellation policy. If you require cancellations through your online portal or to a specific email address, and the cardholder used a different email or sent to the wrong address, you have a stronger position. If your policy says "email us at support@example.com" and they emailed that address, proving non-receipt is difficult. Show your email server logs (no message received) and your portal logs (no cancellation submitted). If you cannot definitively prove no cancellation was received, Amex may side with the cardholder.

**Q: Can I charge a cancellation fee if I don't have a written policy?**
A: No. Without a documented and disclosed cancellation fee policy, retaining any portion of a customer's payment after they cancel creates a C04 that you will lose. Implement a clear written policy before charging cancellation fees.

**Q: Our event was postponed, not cancelled. The cardholder wants a refund but we don't offer them for postponements. Is this a valid C04?**
A: Possibly. If your policy clearly distinguishes postponement from cancellation and states no refund is available for postponements, you may be able to defend the dispute. However, Amex tends to view significant postponements (months into the future) as constructive cancellations from the cardholder's perspective. Review each case individually and consider whether the customer relationship value exceeds the dispute cost.

**Q: A cardholder cancelled a subscription but is disputing charges from before the cancellation. Is that a C04?**
A: No — charges that occurred before the cancellation date are legitimately owed. C04 applies to charges that occurred after a valid cancellation. Show your billing records demonstrating that all charges predated the cancellation request, and this is not a valid C04.

**Q: How do I prevent C04 disputes from subscription cancellations?**
A: Send cancellation confirmations immediately with a reference number, effective date, and last charge date. Make cancellation easy — a one-click or one-email process reduces "I tried to cancel but couldn't" disputes significantly. Send a final billing notification before the last charge explaining it is the final charge and the subscription ends on a specific date. This transparency eliminates most C04 disputes before they reach the chargeback stage.
