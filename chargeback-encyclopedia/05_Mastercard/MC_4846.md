# Mastercard 4846 — Correct Transaction Currency Code Not Provided

## Definition

Mastercard reason code 4846 is issued when a transaction was processed in a currency other than the currency of the country where the transaction took place, or when Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) was applied without proper cardholder consent. In plain terms: the cardholder was charged in a currency they did not agree to, or they were not properly informed that currency conversion was occurring.

This code primarily affects merchants offering Dynamic Currency Conversion services or merchants operating internationally without proper currency code configuration in their payment systems.

## What is Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC)?

Dynamic Currency Conversion is a service that allows international cardholders to pay in their home currency at a foreign point of sale. For example, a US cardholder shopping in Paris can choose to be charged in USD rather than EUR, with the conversion handled at the point of sale rather than by their card issuer.

DCC is an optional service that **requires explicit, informed cardholder consent** before it is applied. Key Mastercard rules:

1. The merchant must inform the cardholder that DCC is being applied
2. The cardholder must actively choose DCC over paying in the local currency
3. The exchange rate and any fees must be disclosed before the cardholder decides
4. The cardholder must be able to decline DCC and pay in local currency
5. The cardholder's choice must be documented

Applying DCC without following these requirements, or applying it automatically without offering the choice, violates Mastercard rules and creates 4846 liability.

## Common Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Automatic DCC Without Consent**
A tourist from the UK uses their card at a Paris restaurant. The POS terminal is configured to automatically apply DCC to all foreign cards, converting the charge to GBP without asking the cardholder. The cardholder notices the GBP charge on their statement, expected EUR, and disputes it under 4846.

**Scenario 2: DCC Not Disclosed at Time of Transaction**
A US cardholder uses their card at a hotel in Japan. The front desk applies DCC and charges in USD. The exchange rate and conversion fee are printed on the receipt in small print, but the cardholder was never verbally informed or asked to consent. This constitutes lack of informed consent and creates 4846 liability.

**Scenario 3: Currency Code Configuration Error**
An e-commerce merchant based in Canada operates a website configured to process all transactions in USD. Canadian customers using Canadian-issued cards are charged in USD without being notified. This creates a currency mismatch and 4846 disputes.

**Scenario 4: Multi-Currency Error**
A merchant with multiple locations operates in both EUR and GBP markets. A configuration error routes UK transactions to a EUR-configured terminal. Cardholders are charged in EUR instead of GBP without consent.

**Scenario 5: DCC Declined, Still Applied**
A cardholder explicitly selects "No DCC — charge in local currency" on a POS terminal. The terminal records the preference but a system error still applies DCC during settlement. This is a technical failure creating 4846 liability.

## Mastercard DCC Rules — Compliance Requirements

To legally offer DCC and defend against 4846 disputes, merchants must comply with Mastercard's DCC framework:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cardholder disclosure | Inform cardholder DCC is available before processing |
| Currency choice | Offer the choice: local currency OR home currency |
| Rate and fee disclosure | Display the exchange rate and any DCC fees before consent |
| Written consent | Capture consent on receipt or digital signature |
| Opt-out option | Cardholder must be able to decline at any time |
| Receipt requirements | Receipt must show local currency amount, home currency amount, exchange rate, and DCC fees |

## Required Evidence

To contest a 4846 chargeback, the merchant must provide:

1. **Signed DCC Consent Record**: The cardholder's signed (paper or digital) confirmation that they consented to DCC, with the exchange rate and fees shown at the time of consent.
2. **POS Transaction Receipt**: Showing both the local currency amount and the home currency amount, the exchange rate applied, and the DCC fee.
3. **DCC Disclosure Documentation**: Evidence of how the DCC option was presented to the cardholder (screen capture, terminal printout, or description of the DCC selection process).
4. **Cardholder Selection Record**: The terminal or system log showing the cardholder actively selected DCC (not just that DCC was applied).
5. **Currency Code in Authorization**: The authorization record showing the correct currency code was transmitted to the issuer.

## Winning Strategy

1. **Present the signed DCC consent form.** The cardholder's signature on a receipt or digital confirmation showing they chose DCC and acknowledged the rate and fees is your primary defense. Without this, 4846 is difficult to win.

2. **Show the full disclosure at point of sale.** If your DCC process requires the cardholder to confirm on a PIN pad or touchscreen, provide screenshots or a description of the customer-facing interface, including what options were presented and in what order.

3. **Document the terminal selection log.** Most DCC-capable terminals log the currency selection event. Provide this log showing the cardholder's active selection rather than a default application.

4. **For currency configuration errors**: Acknowledge the error, provide evidence that it was isolated, and show that a refund or currency correction was applied. This demonstrates good faith and may result in chargeback reversal.

## Losing Mistakes

- **Applying DCC as a default without opt-in**: This is the most common 4846 failure mode. DCC must be opt-in, not opt-out.
- **No signed DCC consent on file**: Without documented cardholder consent, there is no defense.
- **Poor disclosure at point of sale**: Showing the DCC option in fine print on the terminal without actively presenting it to the cardholder does not constitute informed consent.
- **Currency code mismatch in authorization vs. settlement**: If the currency in the authorization request differs from the currency in the settlement, the issuer may flag both a 4846 and an authorization error.
- **DCC applied after the cardholder declined**: If your system records show the cardholder declined DCC, you cannot defend a 4846 chargeback. Process the refund and review the terminal configuration.

## Prevention Checklist

- Configure DCC terminals to require affirmative cardholder selection before applying conversion — never auto-apply.
- Display DCC options prominently on customer-facing terminal screens with clear language in the cardholder's language.
- Print the DCC rate, fee, local amount, and home currency amount on every DCC receipt.
- Retain signed DCC consent records for a minimum of 18 months.
- Audit DCC terminal configuration after every software update to ensure consent workflow has not been altered.
- For e-commerce: present currency selection as a clear, prominent choice before the payment step, and log the selection.
- Train staff on Mastercard's DCC consent requirements and the consequences of non-compliance.
- Periodically test the DCC opt-out path to ensure cardholders can successfully decline and be charged in local currency.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: A cardholder says they did not know DCC was applied. I have a signed receipt showing it was. Do I win?**
A: A signed receipt showing DCC details (rate, converted amount, fees) is strong evidence, but you must also demonstrate that the cardholder actively chose DCC rather than being presented with a pre-selected option. If your terminal's workflow shows an active selection, you have a strong case.

**Q: My e-commerce store only accepts USD but I have international customers. Does this create 4846 risk?**
A: Yes, for non-US cardholders. If your checkout does not disclose that all charges are in USD, international cardholders may dispute the currency. Ensure your checkout clearly states the billing currency, and ideally present a currency selector if you serve international markets.

**Q: Can I offer DCC as the default and let cardholders opt out?**
A: No. Mastercard requires DCC to be opt-in. The local currency must be the default, and DCC must be presented as an option that the cardholder actively chooses. Opt-out models violate Mastercard's DCC rules.

**Q: The cardholder accepted DCC but is now claiming they didn't understand they were being charged in their home currency. Do I still win?**
A: If you have documentation of informed consent (signed receipt, terminal selection log showing the cardholder chose DCC with the rate and fee disclosed), you have a strong defense. The cardholder's post-transaction claim of misunderstanding does not override documented consent, provided the disclosure was genuinely clear.

**Q: Who sets the DCC exchange rate, and can it trigger a chargeback?**
A: DCC rates are set by the DCC service provider, typically a third-party processor. The rate itself cannot be disputed under 4846, but failure to disclose the rate before obtaining consent can. Always show the rate prominently before the cardholder confirms the DCC selection.
