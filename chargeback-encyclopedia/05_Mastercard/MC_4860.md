# Mastercard 4860 — Credit Not Processed

## What This Reason Code Means

Mastercard reason code 4860 — Credit Not Processed — is issued when a cardholder believes they were promised a refund that never appeared on their statement. Unlike fraud-based or delivery-based disputes, 4860 is almost entirely a merchant-side procedural failure. The chargeback lifecycle looks like this:

1. Merchant agrees (explicitly or implicitly) to issue a credit
2. The cardholder waits 15 days and sees no refund
3. The cardholder contacts their bank
4. The bank files a 4860 chargeback

This is the most preventable chargeback code in the Mastercard system. The root cause is almost always one of two scenarios: either the merchant never actually processed the refund, or the refund was processed but to the wrong card, wrong amount, or wrong transaction reference.

---

## The 15-Day Cardholder Wait Period

Mastercard's rules require cardholders to **wait at least 15 calendar days** after requesting a refund before they are eligible to file a 4860 chargeback. This waiting period exists to give merchants time to process credits, which typically clear in 5–10 business days depending on the issuing bank.

This means that if a cardholder files within 15 days, the chargeback can technically be challenged on procedural grounds — but this is rarely a winning strategy if the refund truly has not been processed. Instead, use the window to issue the refund immediately and provide the refund transaction reference to your acquirer to have the dispute closed.

Practical implication: **process every agreed refund the same day it is agreed to.** Do not let refund requests sit in a queue or await manager approval over multiple days. The faster the credit hits the cardholder's account, the lower your 4860 exposure.

---

## Why Merchants Almost Always Lose This Dispute

If a cardholder files a 4860 and the merchant genuinely did not process the refund, there is almost nothing to argue. The issuing bank will verify that no credit transaction appears against the cardholder's account, and the chargeback will stand.

The scenarios in which merchants lose 4860 disputes:

- **Refund never processed** — the most common cause. A staff member forgot, the system glitched, or the request was never escalated
- **Refund processed to wrong card** — the merchant issued a credit to an old card number or a different customer
- **Refund processed for wrong amount** — partial refund issued when full refund was promised
- **Refund was a check or store credit** — unless the cardholder agreed in writing to an alternative form of refund, chargebacks will succeed
- **Refund processed but not yet settled** — if the credit is pending but hasn't cleared within a reasonable window, the cardholder may file anyway

The single scenario in which a merchant can win a 4860 dispute is when the refund **was** issued and the cardholder either did not wait long enough or did not recognize the credit line item on their statement.

## The One Winning Evidence Set

If you did process the refund, your evidence package must contain:

| Evidence | Description |
|---|---|
| **Refund transaction ID** | The unique reference number from your payment processor for the credit transaction |
| **Refund amount and date** | Confirming the full agreed amount was issued on a specific date |
| **Refund confirmation email** | Sent to the cardholder showing the credit was processed |
| **Payment gateway/processor record** | A screenshot or export from your payment platform showing the credit status as "settled" or "processed" |
| **Original transaction reference** | Linking the refund to the specific purchase that was disputed |

A refund transaction ID is the single most powerful piece of evidence. If you can show the issuer that a credit of $X was applied to the cardholder's account on date Y, the dispute is resolved in your favor. Without a transaction ID, you cannot prove the refund occurred.

## The "Store Credit" Problem

A frequent source of 4860 chargebacks is merchants issuing store credit instead of a card refund — without the cardholder's explicit written agreement. Under Mastercard rules, if the original payment was made by card, the refund must go back to the same card unless the cardholder agrees in writing to an alternative.

If you want to offer store credit in lieu of a card refund, you must:
1. Offer it as an option, not a default
2. Obtain the cardholder's written agreement (email confirmation is sufficient)
3. Send the store credit or voucher promptly

If you do all three, the 4860 chargeback can be fought with the written agreement. If you simply applied store credit without consent, you will lose the dispute.

## Proactive Prevention: Building a Zero-4860 Operation

The goal with 4860 is never to respond to one — it should be prevented entirely. Merchants with well-run operations can reduce 4860 chargebacks to near zero.

**Process refunds immediately.** The moment a refund is agreed to — whether via a customer service email, a phone call, or a return portal — process it before the conversation ends or the ticket is closed.

**Send refund confirmation emails automatically.** Your payment platform should trigger a confirmation email with the refund amount, estimated credit timeline (typically 5–10 business days), and a transaction reference number. This sets expectations and gives the cardholder documentation before they escalate.

**Audit refund queues daily.** If your operation uses a ticketing system or approval workflow for refunds, designate a daily audit to ensure no refund requests are sitting unresolved beyond 48 hours.

**Train customer service on refund authority.** Empower front-line agents to process refunds up to a threshold without manager approval. Slow approvals lead to delayed refunds, which lead to 4860 chargebacks.

**Reconcile refunds weekly.** Compare your CRM/order management system's refund records against your payment gateway's transaction log. Any discrepancy — a refund logged in your system but not appearing in the gateway — should be resolved immediately.

## How to Respond If You Receive a 4860

**Step 1.** Verify whether the refund was actually processed by checking your payment gateway. Do not rely on your CRM alone — confirm the credit appears in the settlement records.

**Step 2.** If the refund was processed, gather the refund transaction ID and confirmation details and submit them to your acquirer immediately.

**Step 3.** If the refund was not processed, issue it immediately and notify your acquirer of the refund transaction ID. In many cases, the issuing bank will withdraw the chargeback once they see the credit pending on the cardholder's account.

**Step 4.** Identify the root cause of why the refund was not processed and fix the internal process to prevent recurrence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The customer agreed to store credit but is now filing a 4860. Do I have a defense?**
A: Yes, if you have written proof that the cardholder agreed to store credit instead of a card refund. Submit the email or chat transcript showing their explicit acceptance of the alternative. Without that written agreement, you will likely lose.

**Q: I processed the refund, but the cardholder's bank says they don't see it. What happened?**
A: Credit processing timelines vary by issuing bank — some credits appear within 2–3 business days, while others can take up to 10 business days. Submit your refund transaction ID and the date the credit was processed. The issuer will verify against their system. The chargeback should be resolved in your favor once they confirm the credit.

**Q: Can I fight a 4860 on the grounds that the cardholder filed too soon (before 15 days)?**
A: Technically yes, but it is not advisable unless you were planning to issue the refund anyway and it was genuinely in process. If the refund has not been issued, the dispute will simply cycle — the cardholder will re-file after the waiting period, and you will face the same chargeback again with an added chargeback on your ratio.

**Q: The refund was processed but to the wrong amount. What should I do?**
A: Issue the additional credit for the difference immediately, then submit evidence of both transactions (original credit + supplemental credit) to your acquirer. Acknowledge the error in your rebuttal — issuers respond better to merchants who take responsibility and demonstrate correction than to those who argue over minor shortfalls.

**Q: My system shows the refund as "pending" — is that good enough to fight the chargeback?**
A: Pending status carries less weight than settled status. A pending refund means it was initiated but not yet credited to the cardholder's account. Submit what you have — the initiation timestamp and refund reference — and request an expedited settlement with your payment processor. In the meantime, communicate the pending status to your acquirer.
