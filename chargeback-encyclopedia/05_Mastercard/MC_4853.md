# Mastercard 4853 — Cardholder Dispute

## Definition

Mastercard reason code 4853 is a broad umbrella code covering a wide range of cardholder disputes that do not involve fraud or authorization failures. It is filed when the cardholder has a legitimate complaint about the nature, quality, or fulfillment of goods or services — and the merchant has failed to resolve the issue to the cardholder's satisfaction before the dispute reaches the issuing bank.

4853 encompasses multiple sub-types, each with its own evidence requirements. The merchant's first task when receiving a 4853 is to identify the specific sub-reason (provided by the issuer in the chargeback notice) and build the appropriate response.

## 4853 Sub-Types and Definitions

### Sub-Type 1: Goods or Services Not as Described or Defective

The cardholder received goods or services that materially differ from the merchant's description at the time of purchase, or the goods received were defective/damaged.

**Examples**:
- An online shopper orders a red dress, receives a blue one
- A piece of software described as compatible with macOS does not work on any Mac
- A hotel room described as "ocean view" faces an interior courtyard
- A piece of furniture arrives damaged from shipping

### Sub-Type 2: Goods or Services Not Provided

The cardholder was charged but the goods were never delivered or the services were never rendered. This overlaps with reason code 4855 but may be filed under 4853 in some issuer workflows.

### Sub-Type 3: Credit Not Processed

The merchant agreed to issue a refund (verbally or in writing) but failed to process it within a reasonable timeframe. The cardholder is disputing the charge because the promised credit never appeared on their statement.

### Sub-Type 4: Cancelled Recurring Transaction

The cardholder cancelled a recurring subscription or recurring charge authorization, but the merchant continued to bill them after cancellation.

### Sub-Type 5: Credit Not Processed for Return

The cardholder returned merchandise to the merchant in accordance with the merchant's return policy, but no refund was issued.

### Sub-Type 6: Counterfeit Goods

The goods received were counterfeit — not authentic products as described or implied by the merchant's listing.

## How to Identify the Sub-Type

When you receive a 4853 chargeback, the issuer's chargeback notice will include a sub-reason code or narrative description. Read this carefully before building your response. Submitting evidence for the wrong sub-type will result in a failed Second Presentment.

Common sub-reason indicators in chargeback notices:
- "Merchandise not as described" → Sub-Type 1
- "Services not provided" → Sub-Type 2
- "Credit not issued" → Sub-Type 3 or 5
- "Cancelled recurring" or "Recurring after cancellation" → Sub-Type 4

## Evidence Matrix by Sub-Type

### Sub-Type 1: Not as Described / Defective

| Evidence Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Original product/service description | Prove what was promised |
| Photos of the delivered item | Compare to description |
| Delivery confirmation | Prove delivery occurred |
| Return policy | Show conditions for returns |
| Communication records | Show merchant's response to complaint |
| Expert opinion (for defect claims) | Rebut defect allegation with professional assessment |

**Key argument**: The goods/services substantially matched the description, or the merchant offered a remedy (return, replacement, repair) that the cardholder refused.

### Sub-Type 3: Credit Not Processed

| Evidence Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Refund transaction record | Prove the credit was actually issued |
| Credit date and amount | Show refund was timely |
| Cardholder communication | Show credit notification was sent |
| Return merchandise record | If applicable, proof of return receipt |

**Key argument**: The refund was processed (provide the credit transaction ID and date) and should appear on the cardholder's statement within 3-5 business days.

### Sub-Type 4: Cancelled Recurring

| Evidence Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Recurring billing terms agreed to at signup | Prove the recurring structure |
| Cancellation policy | Show the notice period required |
| Cancellation request date | Establish when cardholder actually cancelled |
| Final billing date | Show billing stopped after the valid notice period |
| Communications about cancellation | Show merchant's response to the cancellation request |

**Key argument**: The billing was within the authorized recurring terms, or the billing date preceded the valid cancellation notice date.

### Sub-Type 5: Credit Not Processed for Return

| Evidence Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Return policy | Prove the conditions under which returns are accepted |
| Return receipt date | When the item was received back |
| Refund processing record | Proof of credit issued |
| No-return evidence | If merchant never received the item, show this |
| Condition-of-goods clause | If returned item was damaged/used beyond policy |

**Key argument**: The refund was issued (provide proof), or the return did not meet the merchant's stated return policy conditions.

## Merchant Liability

Merchant liability under 4853 depends heavily on the sub-type and the merchant's actions:

- **High liability**: Merchant refuses to address a legitimate complaint, fails to process a promised refund, or continues recurring billing after a valid cancellation.
- **Reduced liability**: Merchant can demonstrate the goods matched the description, the refund was processed, or the cancellation request was not received before the billing date.
- **Low or no liability**: Merchant offered an appropriate remedy (replacement, repair, refund) and the cardholder refused it without justification.

## Winning Strategy

1. **Identify the sub-type before writing your rebuttal.** Tailor every piece of evidence and every argument to the specific sub-reason. A generic response covering all scenarios is less effective than a focused response addressing the exact allegation.

2. **For "not as described" disputes: show the description.** Pull screenshots, catalog listings, or contractual descriptions as they existed at the time of purchase. If the goods matched, show this visually and in writing.

3. **For "credit not processed" disputes: lead with the refund record.** If you issued the refund, the refund transaction ID and date is your winning evidence. Present this prominently and the chargeback should be reversed.

4. **For cancelled recurring disputes: document your cancellation process.** Show the cancellation notice period in your terms, the date you received the cancellation request, and that billing stopped on the correct date. If the billing predated the cancellation, clearly demonstrate the timeline.

5. **Show the remedy offered and rejected.** If you offered the cardholder a replacement, repair, or refund and they rejected it, document this. A cardholder who refuses a reasonable remedy and then files a chargeback is engaging in an improper use of the dispute process.

6. **Contact the cardholder first.** Before the chargeback reaches the dispute stage, proactive outreach to dissatisfied customers prevents escalation. Many 4853 chargebacks begin as customer service issues that a responsive merchant could have resolved.

## Losing Mistakes

- **No written refund policy or cancellation policy**: If you cannot show what you promised in terms of returns/cancellations, you cannot hold the cardholder to any standard.
- **Promising a refund and not delivering**: Never tell a customer "I'll process your refund" and then fail to do so. This is the most straightforward way to lose a 4853 Sub-Type 3 dispute.
- **Continuing to bill after a cancellation request**: Implement a system to immediately halt recurring billing upon cancellation request. Delays cost chargebacks.
- **Ignoring customer complaints before they dispute**: A responsive customer service team that resolves complaints quickly prevents most 4853 disputes.
- **Vague product descriptions**: Ambiguous or misleading product descriptions create Sub-Type 1 vulnerability. Be specific and accurate.

## Prevention Checklist

- Write clear, accurate, specific product and service descriptions — avoid superlatives and vague claims.
- Disclose your return, cancellation, and refund policies prominently at checkout.
- Process refunds within 3-5 business days of approval — never delay.
- Build a cancellation workflow that immediately stops recurring billing upon receipt of a cancellation request.
- Confirm returns before processing refunds; confirm refunds were received by the customer.
- Train customer service staff to resolve disputes at the first point of contact.
- Document all customer interactions — every email, chat, and phone note.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The customer returned the item in damaged condition. Can I still lose a 4853?**
A: If your return policy clearly states that damaged items are not eligible for a full refund and this was disclosed at the time of purchase, you can defend based on the condition of the returned goods. Document the condition upon receipt with photographs and reference your stated return policy in your Second Presentment.

**Q: I told the customer their refund would take 10 business days. They disputed before the 10 days were up. What do I do?**
A: Submit the refund processing record showing the refund was initiated before the dispute was filed, along with your refund timeline policy. Many acquirers will accept a chargeback reversal when evidence shows the refund was already in process.

**Q: My cancellation policy requires 30-day advance notice. The customer cancelled on day 28 and is disputing the next charge. Do I win?**
A: If the cancellation policy was clearly disclosed at signup and the customer agreed to it, you may be able to defend the last billing cycle charge. However, any charges after the cancellation effective date (day 28 + 30 days) must stop. Provide the signed terms, the cancellation request date, and the billing date in your Second Presentment.

**Q: Can I fight a "not as described" chargeback if the product photo online was slightly different from the item shipped?**
A: This depends on the degree of difference. A minor, non-material variation (slightly different shade of a color) is defensible with accurate description evidence. A material difference (wrong size, wrong material, wrong product) is much harder to defend. Accurate, detailed product photography and descriptions are your best prevention.
