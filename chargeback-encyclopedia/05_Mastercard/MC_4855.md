# Mastercard 4855 — Goods or Services Not Provided

## What This Reason Code Means

Mastercard reason code 4855 is filed when a cardholder claims they never received the goods or services they paid for. This is one of the most common chargeback codes in e-commerce and is distinct from "not as described" disputes (4853) — the cardholder here is not saying the product was wrong or defective, but that nothing arrived at all.

The dispute can be filed across three broad situations:

- **Physical goods** — a package was never delivered or was delivered to the wrong address
- **Digital goods** — downloadable software, media, or activation keys were not received or could not be accessed
- **Services** — a contracted service (coaching, consulting, cleaning, event tickets) was not performed or fulfilled

The issuing bank typically gives cardholders up to **120 days** from the expected delivery date (not the transaction date) to file this dispute, which creates a long exposure window for merchants — especially those who promise delivery in 30–60 days.

## Merchant Liability: When You Win vs. When You Lose

Understanding where Mastercard places liability is the first step to an effective defense.

### You Are Likely to Win When:

- You have signed proof of delivery (courier signature confirmation, POD document)
- The shipment was delivered to the billing address on the card
- For digital goods, your system logs prove download, activation, or access occurred
- The cardholder placed previous orders and received them — establishing a pattern of successful delivery
- Services were rendered, completed, and acknowledged (client sign-off, project delivery email, meeting records)
- The cardholder contacted you, you resolved the issue, and they filed anyway

### You Are Likely to Lose When:

- You have no tracking number or the tracking shows "in transit" or "undelivered"
- The package was delivered to a different address than the billing address without cardholder authorization
- You cannot prove digital delivery (no server-side logs, no activation confirmation)
- The service was never performed, or you agreed to refund and did not process it
- You missed the agreed delivery date by a material margin and the cardholder canceled before shipment

## Evidence Checklist by Fulfillment Type

### Physical Goods

| Evidence Item | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Carrier tracking number | Proves shipment occurred |
| Delivery confirmation (POD) | Proves the item arrived |
| Signature confirmation | Strongest evidence — named recipient |
| Shipping label showing cardholder's address | Proves delivery to correct location |
| Carrier website screenshot with timestamp | Independent confirmation of delivery status |
| Order confirmation email | Shows buyer acknowledged purchase and address |

When using carriers like FedEx, UPS, or DHL, always request **proof of delivery (POD) letters** — these are official carrier documents that show the time, date, and sometimes the name of the person who signed. Screenshots from carrier websites carry less weight but are acceptable supplementary evidence.

If delivery required a signature, a signed delivery receipt is typically dispositive — the dispute ends there.

### Digital Goods

Digital delivery disputes are harder to fight because there is no physical handoff. Your evidence must come from server-side records:

- **Download logs** — IP address, timestamp, session ID of the download event
- **Activation records** — product key generation and redemption logs
- **Email delivery confirmation** — show the license or download link was emailed and not bounced
- **Login/access records** — if the product is SaaS or a streaming service, session logs showing the cardholder's account was accessed
- **Customer support history** — if they contacted you about the product, they received it

Many digital merchants lose 4855 disputes because they can only show the order was placed, not that the delivery occurred. Invest in robust server-side delivery logging.

### Services

Service disputes require proof of performance:

- **Signed service agreement or contract**
- **Completion certificate or project sign-off**
- **Correspondence confirming service was performed** (emails, messages)
- **Appointment records, attendance logs**
- **Invoices or timesheets** with client-acknowledged milestones

If the service spans multiple sessions (monthly coaching, ongoing consulting), provide records for each session performed up to the dispute date.

## Timeframe Disputes

A common variation of 4855 involves **late delivery** rather than total non-delivery. The cardholder ordered expecting delivery by a certain date (perhaps for an event or holiday), the delivery was late, and they dispute claiming goods were "not provided."

To fight a timeframe dispute:
- Show your website's stated delivery estimate at time of purchase
- Show the cardholder was notified of any delays (email, SMS)
- Show the cardholder did not cancel before shipment
- Provide the actual delivery date from carrier records

If you promised a specific delivery date in writing and missed it, the cardholder has a stronger claim. This is especially common in custom-made goods or B2B orders.

## Proactive Refund Strategy

For 4855 disputes, a proactive refund is often the correct business decision — not a defeat. Consider issuing a refund when:

- You cannot locate a tracking number for the shipment
- Tracking shows the package is lost or undelivered after 7+ days
- The cost of the chargeback fee plus your dispute time exceeds the transaction value
- The customer is clearly acting in good faith

Issuing a refund before the chargeback finalizes prevents the dispute fee, protects your chargeback ratio, and maintains customer goodwill. If you issue a refund after a chargeback is filed, immediately provide the refund transaction reference to your acquirer — this can result in the dispute being withdrawn.

## How to Build Your Rebuttal Letter

A strong rebuttal for 4855 should:

1. State the transaction date, order number, and amount
2. Clearly assert that the goods or services were delivered/rendered
3. Reference each piece of evidence attached, with a one-sentence explanation of what it proves
4. Rebut any specific claims in the chargeback notice
5. Close by requesting the chargeback be reversed

Keep it factual and concise — one to two pages maximum. Avoid emotional language. Acquirers and issuers review hundreds of these; clarity wins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The tracking shows "delivered" but the cardholder says they never got it. Who wins?**
A: In most cases, a carrier confirmation of delivery — especially with a signature — is sufficient for Mastercard to side with the merchant. If the cardholder claims porch theft, that is their claim to substantiate with a police report. Without a police report, the delivery confirmation typically stands.

**Q: Can I fight a 4855 if the order was delivered late but eventually arrived?**
A: Yes, if you can document that the cardholder did not cancel the order before shipment and that they ultimately received the goods. Include the original estimated delivery date, the reason for delay, any notifications sent to the cardholder, and the final delivery confirmation.

**Q: My customer claims they never received a digital download, but my logs show it was accessed. What should I include?**
A: Include server logs showing the download or activation event tied to the cardholder's account, IP address, and timestamp. If the IP address matches prior orders from that customer, include that data as well. Log evidence is generally treated similarly to delivery confirmation for digital goods.

**Q: How long do I have to respond to a 4855 chargeback?**
A: Mastercard's standard merchant response window is **45 days** from the date of the chargeback notification. However, your acquirer may impose a shorter internal deadline — check with them immediately upon receipt.

**Q: Can I win a 4855 if I never shipped the item because I was out of stock?**
A: No — if the item was not shipped and no alternative was offered, the chargeback is valid. You should issue a refund immediately in this scenario. Fighting it will result in a loss and adds a chargeback to your ratio anyway.
