# Mastercard 4808 — Required Authorization Not Obtained

## Definition

Mastercard reason code 4808 is filed when a transaction was settled without a valid authorization from the card issuer, or when the authorization obtained was insufficient, expired, or improperly obtained. Unlike 4807 (which concerns a warned card), 4808 is specifically about the authorization process itself: the merchant either skipped it, bypassed it, or performed it incorrectly.

This is a process compliance code. The cardholder does not need to dispute the charge — the issuing bank can file this chargeback based purely on the absence of a valid authorization record, even if the cardholder made the purchase willingly.

## When This Reason Code Applies

4808 applies across several distinct failure modes:

- **No Authorization Obtained**: The transaction was settled with no authorization request ever made.
- **Authorization Declined, Transaction Processed Anyway**: The merchant received a decline and then submitted the transaction for settlement without obtaining an approval.
- **Expired Authorization**: The merchant obtained authorization but settled the transaction after the authorization had expired (Mastercard authorizations generally expire in 30 days, but hotel/car rental pre-authorizations have specific rules).
- **Voice Authorization Bypassed**: The merchant was required to call for voice authorization (e.g., transaction exceeded floor limit) but did not do so.
- **Incorrect Authorization Amount**: For card-not-present transactions, if the authorized amount materially differs from the settled amount without a proper re-authorization.
- **System Failure Resulting in Forced Transactions**: Merchant processed transactions offline during a system outage without following proper offline authorization procedures.

## Forced Transactions Explained

A forced transaction (also called a "force post") occurs when a merchant submits a transaction for settlement with a manually entered authorization code, bypassing the live authorization network. This is sometimes done:

- When a customer claims they received verbal approval from their bank
- During system outages where the merchant enters a made-up or pre-obtained code
- To push through a transaction that was previously declined

Forced transactions processed without a genuine authorization code are among the most defensible chargebacks for issuers. The merchant has no valid authorization record to present, and the chargeback will almost always stand.

## Common Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Declined Card, Re-swiped and Forced**
A cardholder's card is declined at a restaurant. Rather than asking for another form of payment, the server swipes again without completing a full authorization, and the transaction appears in the next settlement batch. The issuer has no authorization record and files 4808.

**Scenario 2: Expired Hotel Pre-Authorization**
A hotel obtains a pre-authorization for $500 at check-in. The guest extends their stay, and by checkout, the final bill is $820. The hotel settles for $820 without obtaining a new authorization for the full amount. The issuer files a 4808 for the difference or the full amount.

**Scenario 3: POS Outage and Offline Processing**
A merchant's POS goes offline for four hours. The merchant processes transactions using a manual floor limit procedure but does not follow the acquiring bank's offline transaction rules, resulting in transactions settled without authorization codes.

**Scenario 4: Voice Authorization Code Not Entered**
A transaction requires voice authorization (amount exceeds floor limit). The merchant calls for auth, receives a code, but fails to enter it into the POS system before settlement. The transaction settles with no authorization code attached.

## Merchant Liability

Merchant liability under 4808 is **extremely high** in virtually all cases where authorization was genuinely not obtained. Mastercard's rules are clear: a transaction cannot be settled without a valid authorization from the issuer. The burden of proof falls entirely on the merchant to demonstrate a valid authorization existed.

Exceptions where the merchant may have a partial defense:
- Technical failures caused by the acquirer or payment processor (liability may shift to acquirer)
- Documentation of a valid authorization that was not properly linked to the settlement record due to system error

## Required Evidence

To contest a 4808 chargeback, the merchant must provide:

1. **Authorization Approval Code**: The 6-digit code issued by the bank at the time of authorization.
2. **Transaction Log Showing Authorization**: A system record showing the date, time, card number (masked), amount, and approval code.
3. **Voice Authorization Records**: If auth was obtained by phone, document the call time, authorization code, representative name (if available), and the operator ID of the employee who called.
4. **Processor/Gateway Records**: An export from your payment gateway confirming the authorization request and response for the specific transaction.

## Winning Strategy

1. **Immediately pull the authorization record from your payment gateway.** Every legitimate transaction processed through a standard POS or gateway will have an authorization log entry. This is the definitive record.

2. **Match authorization to settlement.** Show that the authorization amount, card number, and date align with the settled transaction. Any mismatch should be explained in your rebuttal letter.

3. **For expired authorization disputes**: Demonstrate you re-authorized before settlement, or show that the time gap between authorization and settlement was within Mastercard's rules for your merchant category (e.g., lodging has extended authorization windows).

4. **For system outage scenarios**: Provide documentation from your acquirer or payment processor confirming the outage, along with your offline authorization procedures and any floor limit agreements.

## Losing Mistakes

- **Submitting only the customer receipt**: A receipt proves a transaction occurred but not that it was authorized. The authorization record is non-negotiable evidence.
- **Arguing the customer made the purchase**: 4808 is not about fraud — it is about process compliance. The cardholder's intent is irrelevant if no authorization exists.
- **Providing an authorization code you cannot verify**: If the authorization code in your system does not match issuer records, your Second Presentment will fail.
- **Missing the 45-day response window**: Authorization disputes are among the most time-sensitive. Missing the deadline results in automatic loss.
- **Force-posting and then attempting to fight**: Without a genuine issuer-generated authorization code, there is no viable defense.

## Prevention Checklist

- Configure your POS to reject transactions where authorization is declined — never re-swipe after a decline.
- Establish a clear offline transaction policy with your acquirer, including floor limits and mandatory call-in thresholds.
- Train staff on the difference between a forced transaction and a properly authorized one.
- For hospitality merchants: re-authorize when the final bill exceeds the pre-authorization by more than your acquirer's tolerance threshold (typically 15-20% plus applicable taxes).
- Audit your settlement reports monthly to identify any transactions settled without matching authorization records.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My terminal showed "Approved" — how can the issuer claim no authorization was obtained?**
A: This usually means the authorization and settlement records are not linked in the issuer's system, often due to a transaction ID mismatch or a forced transaction code override. Check your gateway logs to find the matching authorization and include it in your rebuttal.

**Q: Can I fight a 4808 if the customer admits they made the purchase?**
A: The cardholder's admission is helpful but not sufficient. The chargeback is about process compliance. Include the customer's statement along with any authorization evidence you have, but the authorization record is still required.

**Q: What is the difference between 4807 and 4808?**
A: 4807 is about a card on a warning list — the card was flagged and should not have been accepted. 4808 is about the authorization process — no valid authorization was obtained regardless of whether the card was flagged. Both relate to authorization, but for different underlying reasons.

**Q: How long are Mastercard authorizations valid?**
A: Standard retail authorizations expire after 30 days. Lodging and vehicle rental authorizations follow extended completion timeframes per Mastercard's merchant category-specific rules. Always re-authorize if settlement is delayed.
