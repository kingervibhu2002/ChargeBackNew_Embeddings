# Mastercard 4837 — No Cardholder Authorization

## Definition

Mastercard reason code 4837 is the network's primary fraud-related chargeback code, and it is the most frequently filed Mastercard dispute code. It is issued when a cardholder claims they did not authorize a transaction charged to their account — meaning someone used their card or card credentials without their knowledge or consent.

4837 covers both true fraud (stolen card, account takeover, phishing) and friendly fraud (the cardholder actually made the purchase but denies it to obtain a refund). Distinguishing between the two and building the appropriate evidence package is the central challenge of 4837 defense.

## When This Reason Code Applies

4837 is filed when:

- **CNP Fraud**: A fraudster uses stolen card credentials to make an online, phone, or mail-order purchase without the physical card.
- **Physical Card Fraud**: A stolen or counterfeit card (excluding EMV counterfeit, which has its own code) is used at a card-present terminal.
- **Account Takeover**: A fraudster gains access to the cardholder's online account and makes purchases.
- **Friendly Fraud**: The cardholder made the purchase themselves but falsely claims they did not to obtain a refund or free goods.
- **Family Fraud**: A family member (often a child) used the cardholder's credentials without explicit permission.

## Types of 4837 Fraud and Their Characteristics

### True CNP Fraud
- Card credentials were obtained through phishing, data breach, or dark web purchase
- Order shipping address differs from the cardholder's billing address
- Multiple orders placed in rapid succession
- First-time customer with no prior purchase history
- High-value items (electronics, gift cards) ordered for rapid resale

### Physical Card Fraud
- Card was reported lost or stolen around the transaction date
- Cardholder was in a different geographic location at the time of the transaction
- Multiple transactions at different locations in an implausibly short timeframe

### Friendly Fraud
- Cardholder has prior purchase history with the merchant for similar items
- Shipping address matches the cardholder's billing address
- Account login from the cardholder's known device and IP address
- No report of card loss or theft around the transaction date
- Cardholder contacts merchant about the order before the dispute (order status inquiry, delivery questions)

## Compelling Evidence Standard for 4837

Mastercard requires that Second Presentments for 4837 meet a **compelling evidence standard** — the evidence must affirmatively demonstrate that the cardholder was the one who made the purchase, not merely that the merchant followed standard procedures.

### For Card-Not-Present Transactions

The strongest evidence package includes:

1. **3D Secure (Mastercard Identity Check) Authentication**: If the transaction was authenticated through Mastercard Identity Check (3DS 2.0), liability shifts to the issuer. This is the single most powerful defense for CNP fraud.

2. **AVS Match**: The billing address provided at checkout matched the issuer's records. AVS Full Match (Y response) indicates the cardholder's address was verified.

3. **CVV2 Match**: The CVV2 code was presented and matched. This proves physical access to the card or card credentials.

4. **IP Address and Device Fingerprint**: The order was placed from an IP address and device consistent with the cardholder's known location and device profile.

5. **Delivery Confirmation to Billing Address**: Proof of delivery to the cardholder's verified billing address, including signature if available.

6. **Prior Purchase History**: Records showing the cardholder has purchased from your store before using the same credentials.

7. **Customer Account Login Records**: If the order was placed through a registered account, logs showing login from the cardholder's email, password, and known device.

8. **Order Confirmation Email Opened**: Email open/click tracking showing the order confirmation was opened from the cardholder's known email account and device.

### For Card-Present Transactions

1. **EMV Chip Transaction Data**: If the chip was read (not swiped), the cryptographic transaction data significantly reduces fraud liability.
2. **PIN Verification**: If PIN was used, liability shifts to the issuer.
3. **Signed Sales Receipt**: Cardholder signature matching the card's signature panel.
4. **Security Camera Footage**: Where available, footage of the cardholder at the point of sale.

## Mastercard Identity Check (3DS) — The Liability Shield

When a merchant enables Mastercard Identity Check and a transaction is fully authenticated, **liability for 4837 disputes shifts to the issuing bank**. The issuer cannot file a valid 4837 chargeback on a successfully authenticated transaction.

Partial authentication (attempted but not completed) provides partial liability shift in some scenarios. Merchants should work with their payment gateway to enable full 3DS 2.0 authentication for all card-not-present transactions.

## Friendly Fraud Identification and Defense

Friendly fraud is increasingly common — estimated at 40-80% of all fraud chargebacks in some merchant categories. Key indicators and defenses:

| Indicator | Defense Evidence |
|---|---|
| Repeat customer | Order history report showing prior purchases |
| Delivery to billing address | Carrier tracking with delivery to that specific address |
| No card theft report | Absence of fraud report around transaction date |
| Subsequent purchases after disputed transaction | Order history showing continued shopping |
| Customer service contact about the order | Chat logs, email threads, phone records referencing the order |
| Social media posts showing receipt of the item | Publicly available posts (screenshots) |

## Required Evidence Package

For a complete 4837 Second Presentment, compile:

1. **Transaction summary** (amount, date, authorization code, order ID)
2. **3DS authentication result** (if applicable — ECI code, authentication value)
3. **AVS response code** (Y = Full match, Z = ZIP only, etc.)
4. **CVV2 match indicator** (M = Match)
5. **Delivery confirmation** (carrier tracking number, delivery date, recipient name/signature)
6. **IP address, device fingerprint, and geolocation data** at time of order
7. **Customer account registration and login history** (if applicable)
8. **Prior purchase history** from the same account/card
9. **Customer communication records** (emails, chats, support tickets)
10. **Rebuttal letter** synthesizing all evidence and explaining why the cardholder is the likely purchaser

## Winning Strategy

1. **Authenticate via 3DS before the transaction.** This is the only way to guarantee a 4837 defense. All other evidence is probabilistic; 3DS authentication is definitively liability-shifting.

2. **Build a cumulative evidence case.** No single piece of evidence wins a 4837 dispute. The goal is to stack multiple data points — AVS match + CVV match + delivery to billing address + prior purchase history — that collectively make it implausible that the cardholder did not authorize the transaction.

3. **Document shipping to the billing address.** Merchants who ship to the verified billing address are significantly better positioned than those who ship to third-party or freight forwarder addresses.

4. **Use device fingerprinting and IP geolocation.** These tools, when implemented, provide transaction-level data showing that the order was placed from the cardholder's known device and location — powerful evidence against fraud claims.

5. **Leverage customer service records.** Any pre-dispute communication from the cardholder about the order (order status, delivery question, return request) demonstrates they were aware of and engaged with the purchase.

## Losing Mistakes

- **No 3DS authentication for CNP transactions**: This is the single most costly oversight. CNP merchants without 3DS have minimal fraud protection.
- **Shipping to freight forwarders or unverified addresses**: These shipments are difficult to defend because delivery to the cardholder's address cannot be proven.
- **No AVS or CVV verification**: Failing to use available verification tools weakens your evidence package.
- **Generic rebuttal letters**: Submitting a boilerplate response without tailored evidence is ineffective. Each 4837 response must be specific to the transaction.
- **Ignoring friendly fraud patterns**: If a specific customer consistently disputes purchases, flag them in your fraud system and consider blocking future transactions.
- **No IP or device logging**: Without transaction-level technical data, you cannot prove who placed the order.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The order was delivered and signed for. Why did I still lose?**
A: Delivery proof alone is not sufficient for 4837. You need to prove the cardholder was the recipient. If the item was delivered to their confirmed billing address and signed by someone at that address, it is strong evidence. If delivered to a different address, the evidence is weaker.

**Q: Can I fight a 4837 if I have no 3DS and no AVS match?**
A: Yes, but your chances are lower. Compile every other piece of evidence — device fingerprint, IP geolocation, prior order history, delivery proof, customer service records — to build the strongest possible cumulative case.

**Q: What ECI codes indicate successful 3DS authentication?**
A: ECI 02 (Mastercard) indicates fully authenticated. ECI 01 indicates attempted authentication (partial liability shift). ECI 00 indicates no authentication (no liability shift).

**Q: A customer disputed 5 transactions over 6 months. Is this friendly fraud?**
A: This pattern is highly consistent with serial friendly fraud. Document all 5 disputes, your evidence for each, and consider filing a Mastercard Excessive Chargeback report. Block the cardholder from future purchases. Each dispute must still be addressed on its own merits with the evidence available.

**Q: Does a CVV match mean the cardholder authorized the transaction?**
A: A CVV match proves that whoever placed the order had physical access to the card (or card credentials including the CVV). Combined with AVS match and other evidence, it is a strong indicator of cardholder authorization, but it is not conclusive on its own.
