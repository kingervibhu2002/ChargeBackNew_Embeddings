# Mastercard 4849 — Questionable Merchant Activity

## Definition

Mastercard reason code 4849 is one of the most severe chargeback codes in the Mastercard system. It is issued when Mastercard determines that a merchant has engaged in or is associated with activities that violate Mastercard's standards, including merchants who are on the MATCH (Member Alert to Control High-Risk Merchants) list, merchants under Mastercard's chargeback monitoring programs, or merchants whose transaction patterns raise systemic fraud or compliance concerns.

Unlike most chargeback codes that arise from individual transaction disputes, 4849 reflects a network-level determination about the merchant's overall conduct or compliance status. It signals that Mastercard — not just an individual cardholder — has concerns about the merchant.

## When This Reason Code Applies

4849 is triggered by:

- **MATCH List Status**: The merchant or a principal of the business is listed on the MATCH database, which is checked by acquirers when boarding new merchants. Transactions processed by a MATCH-listed entity can be reversed under 4849.
- **Mastercard Chargeback Monitoring Programs**: A merchant enrolled in the Excessive Chargeback Program (ECP) or High Excessive Chargeback Program (HECP) who continues to process without a remediation plan may face 4849 chargebacks.
- **Prohibited Business Activities**: The merchant is engaged in a business activity that Mastercard has classified as prohibited (e.g., certain categories of gambling, adult content, or firearms transactions processed in violation of acquirer/network policies).
- **Regulatory or Legal Violations**: The merchant is under regulatory sanction, court order, or law enforcement action that affects their right to process payments.
- **Fraudulent Business Practices**: Systematic consumer fraud — fake products, advance fee schemes, fraudulent subscriptions — that has been reported to Mastercard or identified by its monitoring systems.
- **Processing Violations**: Systematic violations of Mastercard operating rules beyond individual transaction errors.

## MATCH List — What Triggers It

The MATCH database is maintained by Mastercard and shared across the payment industry. An acquirer is required to list a merchant on MATCH when terminating a merchant account for cause. Listing triggers include:

| MATCH Reason Code | Description |
|---|---|
| 01 | Account Data Compromise |
| 02 | Common Point of Purchase (fraud source) |
| 03 | Laundering/Factoring |
| 04 | Excessive Chargebacks |
| 05 | Excessive Fraud |
| 06 | Reserved |
| 07 | Fraud Conviction |
| 08 | Mastercard Questionable Merchant Audit Program |
| 09 | Bankruptcy/Insolvency |
| 10 | Violation of Standards |
| 11 | Merchant Collusion |
| 12 | PCI-DSS Non-Compliance |
| 13 | Illegal Transactions |
| 14 | Identity Theft |

Once a merchant is on MATCH, their principals (owners, officers, key personnel) are also listed by name. This effectively blocks the merchant from obtaining a new account at most acquiring banks for 5 years.

## Mastercard Monitoring Programs

Merchants who accumulate excessive chargebacks are enrolled in Mastercard's monitoring framework:

### Excessive Chargeback Program (ECP)
- Threshold: More than 100 chargebacks AND chargeback ratio above 1.0% in any month
- Consequence: Required remediation plan, possible fines

### High Excessive Chargeback Program (HECP)
- Threshold: More than 300 chargebacks AND ratio above 1.5% in any month
- Consequence: Monthly fines starting at $1,000, escalating based on duration and severity; risk of MATCH listing

Merchants who remain in the HECP for extended periods without demonstrating improvement face forced account termination and MATCH listing, which then enables 4849 chargebacks on their account.

## Merchant Defense Options — Very Limited

4849 is unique in that the merchant's defense options are severely constrained:

### If MATCH-Listed
The merchant was MATCH-listed because a prior acquirer terminated them for cause. The dispute is fundamentally about the merchant's fitness to process, not about an individual transaction. Defense requires:
1. Challenging the accuracy of the MATCH listing (if it was made in error)
2. Demonstrating the listing reason has been resolved (e.g., the PCI compliance issue has been remediated)
3. Working with the acquirer to formally challenge the listing through Mastercard's dispute process

If the MATCH listing is accurate, there is virtually no transaction-level defense available.

### If in a Monitoring Program
If the merchant is in the ECP or HECP, the focus must be on demonstrating active chargeback reduction efforts:
1. Documented remediation plan
2. Fraud prevention measures implemented
3. Month-over-month chargeback ratio improvement
4. Evidence of cardholder dispute resolution processes

### If Alleged Prohibited Activity
If the 4849 is based on prohibited business activities, the merchant must:
1. Demonstrate that the activities in question are actually permitted under their merchant category and acquiring agreement
2. Show compliance with applicable laws and regulations
3. Provide evidence of the merchant category registration and acquirer approval for the business type

## Required Evidence

Given 4849's systemic nature, evidence must address the root cause:

1. **Current MATCH status documentation**: Request a MATCH database check through your acquirer to confirm your current status.
2. **Remediation plan documentation**: If in a monitoring program, the formal remediation plan submitted to your acquirer.
3. **Chargeback trend reports**: Month-over-month data showing chargeback volume and ratio improvement.
4. **Compliance certificates**: PCI-DSS compliance certificate, relevant business licenses, regulatory approvals.
5. **Business legitimacy documentation**: Business registration, tax ID, physical business address verification, and any relevant industry certifications.
6. **Legal/regulatory clearance**: If the 4849 relates to a legal or regulatory matter, relevant court orders, regulatory correspondence, or counsel letters demonstrating the matter is resolved.

## Winning Strategy

1. **Understand the specific basis for the 4849.** Contact your acquiring bank immediately to determine precisely why 4849 chargebacks are being filed. Is it MATCH status? Monitoring program enrollment? Prohibited activity? The defense strategy depends entirely on the specific basis.

2. **Address the root cause, not the symptom.** Fighting individual 4849 chargebacks without resolving the underlying business compliance issue will not stop the flow of chargebacks. Systemic remediation is required.

3. **Engage payment industry counsel.** 4849 matters frequently involve complex regulatory, contractual, and network rule issues that require legal expertise. Merchants facing 4849 chargebacks should consult an attorney with payment industry experience.

4. **Cooperate fully with your acquirer.** The acquirer is your advocate within the network but also your compliance gatekeeper. Full cooperation with their investigation and remediation requirements is essential.

5. **Challenge inaccurate MATCH listings.** If you have been erroneously MATCH-listed, you have the right to challenge the listing. Work with the original acquirer to request removal, or escalate to Mastercard if the acquirer is unresponsive.

## Losing Mistakes

- **Continuing to process without addressing the compliance issue**: 4849 chargebacks will continue as long as the underlying compliance issue persists.
- **Switching acquirers without disclosure**: Attempting to move processing to a new acquirer without disclosing MATCH status or monitoring program enrollment is a violation of Mastercard rules and will be discovered.
- **Fighting chargebacks individually without engaging the acquirer**: 4849 cannot be resolved chargeback-by-chargeback. The acquirer and Mastercard are the decision-makers.
- **Ignoring communications from the acquiring bank**: An acquirer who has enrolled a merchant in a monitoring program will send formal notices. Ignoring these accelerates the path to account termination.

## Prevention Checklist

- Monitor your chargeback ratio monthly and act proactively if approaching the 1.0% ECP threshold.
- Implement a robust fraud prevention program including 3DS, AVS, CVV, and velocity checks.
- Ensure your business activities, merchant category code, and processing patterns are aligned and disclosed to your acquirer.
- Never process transactions for third parties (no factoring/laundering).
- Maintain PCI-DSS compliance and renew your compliance certificate annually.
- Resolve all cardholder disputes promptly to reduce chargeback escalations.
- Maintain clear, accurate business information with your acquiring bank at all times.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I received a 4849 chargeback but I am not on the MATCH list. How is this possible?**
A: 4849 is broader than just MATCH status. It also covers Mastercard monitoring program enrollment, prohibited activities, and other questionable merchant patterns. Contact your acquirer to determine the specific basis for the 4849 in your case.

**Q: How do I check if I am on the MATCH list?**
A: You can request a MATCH self-inquiry through your acquiring bank or through Mastercard's MATCH inquiry process. Acquiring banks routinely check MATCH before boarding new merchants, and a MATCH hit results in automatic denial of the merchant account application.

**Q: My former business partner was listed on MATCH. Does that affect me?**
A: Potentially. MATCH listings include the principals of the business. If you were listed as a principal of the terminated merchant, the listing attaches to your personal information (name, SSN in the US). This can affect your ability to obtain a merchant account as a principal in a new business.

**Q: Can I negotiate my way off the MATCH list?**
A: Only the acquirer that listed you can remove the listing, and only in specific circumstances (clerical error or resolution of the underlying issue, depending on the listing reason). There is no general negotiation process. Consult a payments attorney for MATCH removal strategies specific to your situation.
