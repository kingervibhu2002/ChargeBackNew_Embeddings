# Mastercard 4871 — Chip and PIN Liability Shift

## What This Reason Code Means

Mastercard reason code 4871 addresses a specific subset of the EMV liability shift framework: transactions where a chip-and-PIN card was used at a chip-capable terminal, but the terminal only required a signature rather than a PIN. The fraud type associated with 4871 is **lost or stolen card fraud** — a criminal obtained the physical card and used it at a point of sale.

This is distinct from reason code 4870 (counterfeit fraud) in both the fraud mechanism and the liability condition:

| | 4870 | 4871 |
|---|---|---|
| Fraud type | Counterfeit (cloned card) | Lost or stolen (physical card) |
| Card element exploited | Magnetic stripe data | Physical card possession |
| Liability trigger | Terminal did not read chip | Terminal read chip but did not require PIN |
| Preventive measure | EMV chip reader | PIN pad required for PIN-capable cards |

The core principle of 4871: if a criminal has a stolen chip card and the merchant requires PIN entry, the criminal is stopped because they do not know the PIN. If the merchant accepts a signature instead, the criminal simply signs — and the merchant bears the resulting fraud loss.

---

## Chip-and-PIN vs. Chip-and-Signature: The Critical Difference

Not all chip cards are the same. There are two primary verification methods for chip transactions:

**Chip-and-PIN (CVM: PIN):** The card is inserted, the chip is read, and the cardholder must enter their 4–6 digit PIN to complete the transaction. The PIN is verified either by the chip itself (offline PIN verification) or by the card network (online PIN verification). A lost or stolen card is useless to a criminal who does not know the PIN.

**Chip-and-Signature (CVM: Signature):** The card is inserted, the chip is read, and the cardholder signs a receipt. Signing requires no secret knowledge — anyone holding the card can sign. A criminal with a stolen chip card can complete a chip-and-signature transaction easily.

Most Mastercard-branded cards issued outside the United States are chip-and-PIN. Within the United States, chip-and-signature has historically been more common, though chip-and-PIN adoption is increasing.

The liability shift under 4871 applies when:
1. The card is a chip-and-PIN enabled card (the card's CVM list includes PIN)
2. The merchant's terminal did not support PIN entry, OR supported PIN but was configured to accept signature instead
3. A lost or stolen card fraud transaction occurred at that terminal

## PIN Pad Configuration and Merchant Liability

Many merchants invest in chip-capable terminals but configure them — intentionally or by default — to accept signature as the primary cardholder verification method (CVM). This configuration is common in retail environments where speed of checkout is prioritized.

The liability problem: if a chip-and-PIN card is used at a terminal configured for signature-only, the terminal "downgrades" the CVM from PIN to signature. The transaction is processed, the stolen card works, and the 4871 chargeback follows.

**Terminal configuration audit questions:**

- Does your terminal have an integrated PIN pad (or is it swipe-and-signature only)?
- Is PIN entry enabled in your terminal's CVM configuration?
- What happens when a chip-and-PIN card is inserted — does the terminal prompt for PIN or automatically request signature?
- Is PIN bypassed or skipped when a cashier presses a "bypass" or "skip" button?

If your terminal has a PIN pad but staff routinely bypass PIN entry to speed up transactions, you still face 4871 liability — the PIN capability existed but was not used.

## When the Merchant Is Not Liable

The merchant successfully avoids 4871 liability in these scenarios:

1. **PIN was entered and verified** — the authorization record contains a CVM result showing successful online or offline PIN verification. If the PIN was correctly entered, the transaction is protected even if the card was stolen (the criminal knew the PIN, which is the cardholder's security responsibility).

2. **The card does not support PIN** — if the card's chip contains no PIN application (chip-and-signature-only card), the terminal cannot be expected to request a PIN. In this case, the issuer bears the liability, not the merchant.

3. **The transaction was below the CVM floor limit** — many chip cards are configured with a minimum transaction amount below which PIN or signature is not required (contactless or no-CVM transactions). Small transactions below this limit (often $25 or less in the US) may not trigger PIN requirements, and the issuer bears the low-value fraud liability.

## Authorization Record Evidence for 4871

As with 4870, the key evidence for 4871 disputes is embedded in the authorization record, not in documents you create after the fact. Your acquirer can provide the full authorization record showing:

- **POS Entry Mode** — confirms chip was read (not swiped)
- **CVM Result** — the cardholder verification method actually used (PIN verified, signature obtained, no CVM)
- **PIN Verification Result** — if PIN was used, whether it was verified online or offline and whether it passed

If the CVM Result shows "PIN verified" and the PIN result shows successful verification, you have a strong defense. If the CVM Result shows "Signature obtained" or "No CVM performed," the terminal accepted a non-PIN verification on a PIN-capable card, and liability shifts to you.

---

## Prevention: Requiring PIN at Your Terminals

The most effective prevention for 4871 is configuring your terminals to require PIN when the inserted card's chip indicates PIN is the required CVM. Steps:

1. **Verify your terminal has a PIN pad** — an integrated or separate PIN entry device (PED) that meets PCI-DSS hardware requirements
2. **Configure PIN as the preferred CVM** in your terminal's payment application settings
3. **Disable signature bypass** — remove or restrict any "skip PIN" or "bypass CVM" functionality available to cashiers
4. **Test with a chip-and-PIN card** — insert a chip-and-PIN card and confirm the terminal prompts for PIN rather than signature
5. **Contact your acquirer or terminal vendor** for EMV configuration assistance — many merchants set this up incorrectly or leave it at default

Note: contactless (tap-to-pay) transactions above certain limits in many countries do require PIN entry. Configure your contactless limits appropriately for your average transaction size.

---

## The Intersection of 4871 and Cross-Border Transactions

4871 exposure increases significantly for merchants in countries where chip-and-signature terminals are common (like the United States) who process cards issued in countries where chip-and-PIN is universal (like the UK, EU, and most of Asia-Pacific). International cardholders using their PIN-enabled cards in US signature-based terminals create liability exposure with every lost-or-stolen fraud event.

If your customer base includes a significant share of international cardholders, PIN pad configuration is especially important — the mismatch between their PIN-enabled cards and your signature terminals puts every lost-or-stolen transaction on your tab.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My terminal has a PIN pad, but my staff skip PIN when customers seem to be in a hurry. Are we liable for 4871?**
A: Yes. If the terminal's configuration allowed PIN bypass and staff used it, the authorization record will show signature or no-CVM as the verification method. The fact that the PIN pad hardware existed is not sufficient — the PIN must actually have been verified. Disable bypass functionality or implement policy controls restricting its use.

**Q: A customer inserted their chip card, entered a PIN, but the PIN was their own card so the criminal must have known it. Are we liable?**
A: No. If the PIN was correctly entered and verified, the 4871 liability shift does not apply. The transaction has the strongest possible authentication — chip plus PIN. The cardholder's claim would need to be evaluated under different grounds. PIN-verified chip transactions are among the most fraud-resistant transactions in the payment ecosystem.

**Q: The card had no PIN (chip-and-signature only). The fraud happened. Can the issuer file a 4871?**
A: No. 4871 specifically requires that the card support PIN verification and that the terminal failed to use it. If the card's chip contains no PIN application, the CVM failure is not the merchant's responsibility. Verify the card type by requesting the authorization record and CVM capability data from your acquirer.

**Q: Can 4871 apply to contactless transactions?**
A: Yes, in certain contexts. If a contactless transaction above the no-CVM floor limit was completed without PIN verification on a PIN-capable card, and lost-or-stolen fraud occurred, 4871 liability can apply. Contactless transactions above the limit should trigger PIN or signature as required by the card's CVM list and your terminal's configuration.

**Q: We serve a lot of tourist customers from Europe. How much 4871 risk do we have if we use signature terminals?**
A: Elevated. European cards are almost universally chip-and-PIN. Each lost or stolen card fraud transaction from a European cardholder at your signature-accepting terminal creates 4871 exposure. If you regularly process high-value transactions with international customers, upgrading to PIN-capable terminals is a high-priority investment with a measurable return in avoided chargeback losses.
