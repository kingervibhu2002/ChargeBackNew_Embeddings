# Rebuttal Template: Mastercard Reason Code 4853 — Cardholder Dispute (Services Not Provided)

## About This Reason Code

Mastercard Reason Code 4853 is a broad cardholder dispute code that covers several scenarios, including goods or services not as described, counterfeit goods, and — in this template's focus — services that were not rendered or were rendered but the cardholder claims they were not received. The services not rendered variant is common for professional services, consulting, event services, maintenance contracts, and custom work orders.

To win a 4853 Second Presentment for services not rendered, you must produce affirmative proof that the service was completed: delivery receipts, completion certificates, sign-offs, meeting records, work logs, or customer acknowledgments. Simply having an invoice is not sufficient.

---

## Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] Service agreement or contract signed by the cardholder
- [ ] Scope of work or statement of work (SOW) document
- [ ] Service delivery records (work logs, session notes, reports)
- [ ] Completion certificate or delivery acknowledgment signed by client
- [ ] Customer communication log (emails, messages confirming service delivery)
- [ ] Invoice and payment authorization record
- [ ] Any customer sign-off at project milestones
- [ ] Photos or recordings of service delivery (if applicable)

## Merchant Notes

- Begin requiring written sign-offs at project milestones for all engagements over $500. A single email from the client saying "looks good" is admissible evidence.
- For recurring service contracts (monthly retainers), keep monthly delivery summaries and send them to the client. An invoice alone cannot prove services were rendered — activity reports can.
- If the client raised concerns before the chargeback and you resolved them, include that resolution chain. If you were not given an opportunity to cure, state so explicitly in your letter.
- 4853 for "not as described" (a variant of this code) requires you to show that the service matched the description in the agreement. Align your evidence to what was specifically promised in writing.
